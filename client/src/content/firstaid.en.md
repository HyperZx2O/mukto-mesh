# First Aid — Crisis Edition

This is not a substitute for professional medical training. In a crisis, do what you can with what you have.

## Tear Gas / Chemical Exposure

Tear gas is commonly used during protests in Bangladesh.

- **Do not rub your eyes or face.**
- Move to higher ground if possible — tear gas settles low.
- Face the wind to clear the air around you.
- Rinse eyes with clean water or saline for 10-15 minutes.
- Wash exposed skin with soap and water. Do **not** use oil-based products — they trap the chemicals.
- Remove and isolate contaminated clothing.
- If breathing becomes difficult, seek medical help immediately.

## Crowd Crush / Stampede

- **Protect your chest:** Cross your arms in front of your chest to keep space to breathe.
- Move with the crowd, not against it.
- If you fall, curl into a fetal position (knees to chest, hands over head) to protect vital organs.
- Help others up if you can.
- Once clear, check for injuries: difficulty breathing, chest pain, bruising.

### CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

If someone is unconscious and not breathing:

1. Call for help — send someone to find a medical professional.
2. Place the person on their back on a firm surface.
3. Kneel beside them, place the heel of one hand on the centre of their chest, other hand on top.
4. Push hard and fast: 2 to 2.4 inches (5-6 cm) deep, 100-120 compressions per minute.
5. Continue until help arrives or the person starts breathing.

## Burns

### Thermal Burns
- Cool the burn under **cool** (not cold) running water for at least 10 minutes.
- Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste, or oil.
- Cover loosely with a clean cloth or sterile gauze.
- Do not pop blisters — they protect against infection.

### Chemical Burns
- Brush off dry chemicals before flushing.
- Flush with cool running water for at least 20 minutes.
- Remove contaminated clothing while flushing.
- Seek medical help for all chemical burns.

## Heat Exhaustion vs Heat Stroke

| Symptom | Heat Exhaustion | Heat Stroke |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Cool, moist, pale | Hot, red, dry |
| Temperature | Normal or slightly high | Very high (40°C+) |
| Mental state | Dizzy, weak, confused | Confused, unconscious, seizure |
| Sweating | Heavy | Stops |

**Heat stroke is a medical emergency — call 999 immediately.** While waiting:
- Move the person to shade or a cool area.
- Remove excess clothing.
- Cool them with wet cloths or ice packs on neck, armpits, and groin.
- Do not give fluids if unconscious.

## Bleeding and Wounds

- Apply direct pressure with a clean cloth or bandage.
- Elevate the injured area above heart level if possible (note: current evidence on this is mixed, but it may help reduce blood flow).
- Do not remove an object that is embedded in the wound — apply pressure around it.
- If blood soaks through, place another bandage on top — do not remove the first.
- Clean minor wounds with clean water and cover with a sterile dressing.

## Fractures and Sprains

- Do not move the injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
- Immobilise the injured limb using a splint (board, rolled newspaper, or sturdy object) and cloth strips.
- Apply ice or a cold pack wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling.
- Seek medical help.

## Panic Attacks

In a crisis, panic attacks are common. If someone is hyperventilating, trembling, or feels like they are dying:

- Speak calmly and stay with them.
- Guide them to breathe slowly: inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4.
- Use the **5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique**:
  - Name 5 things you can see
  - 4 things you can touch
  - 3 things you can hear
  - 2 things you can smell
  - 1 thing you can taste
- Do not say "calm down." Say "I am here. You are safe. Breathe with me."

## Dehydration and Water Safety

- Drink clean water regularly — do not wait until you are thirsty.
- In a shutdown, tap water may still be safe but store enough for 3 days.
- If water is questionable, boil for at least 1 minute or use chlorine tablets.
- Oral rehydration solution: 1 litre clean water + 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt.

## First Aid Kit Essentials

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Sterile gauze pads | Wound dressing |
| Bandages (various sizes) | Covering wounds |
| Antiseptic wipes | Cleaning wounds |
| Pain relievers (paracetamol, ibuprofen) | Fever, pain |
| Oral rehydration salts | Dehydration |
| Burn cream | Minor burns |
| Scissors, tweezers | Cutting, splinter removal |
| Medical gloves | Protection |
| Face mask | Smoke, tear gas |
| Saline solution | Eye washing |
