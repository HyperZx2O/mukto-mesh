# July 2024 — What We Learned

This is the story of why Mukto Mesh exists.

## The Internet Shutdown

In July 2024, the Bangladesh government imposed a near-total internet shutdown that lasted from July 18 to July 23 — five days that cut off 170 million people from the outside world.

It was not a technical failure. It was a deliberate, state-ordered kill switch.

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| June 2024 | Supreme Court reinstates quota system. Students begin peaceful protests. |
| July 1-15 | Protests escalate. Chhatra League attacks on student demonstrators. |
| July 16 | Abu Sayed, a student of Begum Rokeya University, is shot dead by police. |
| July 17 | Violence intensifies. Social media platforms (Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram) are blocked. |
| July 18-23 | Complete nationwide internet shutdown. 170 million people disconnected. |
| July 24-28 | Internet restored partially — slow, unstable, limited to mobile data. |
| August 4-5 | Second shutdown. Non-Cooperation Movement. Sheikh Hasina resigns. |

## What the Shutdown Cost

| Sector | Impact |
|---|---|
| Economy | Estimated loss of over $1 billion per day |
| Banking | ATMs ran dry. Digital payments stopped. |
| Healthcare | Telemedicine cut. Emergency response crippled. |
| Media | News outlets could not publish. Rumours spread unchecked. |
| VPN usage | VPN demand surged 3,000% in 48 hours. Many stopped working. |

## How the Shutdown Was Enforced

- **Verbal directives** — no written order was ever published.
- **NTMC and BTRC** pressured all ISPs and mobile operators to block traffic.
- **VPN blocking** — known VPN protocols were identified and throttled.
- **Social media blocks** — Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram, and YouTube were blocked first, then all internet.

## What Students Did

When the internet died, students in Dhaka and across the country improvised:

- They shared updates through Bluetooth and local WiFi networks.
- They used mesh messaging apps to coordinate across barricades.
- They passed USB drives with news bulletins from neighbourhood to neighbourhood.
- They built small LAN networks in dorms and apartment buildings.

This improvised network was called **Jogajog** — the Bengali word for "communication." It was fragile, slow, and limited in range, but it was something. It kept people alive.

## Lessons Learned

### What Failed
- **Single-platform dependency:** Everyone relied on Facebook and WhatsApp. When they went down, there was no backup.
- **No offline infrastructure:** There was no preloaded app or local server that could function without the internet.
- **Centralised internet access:** The government could cut everything at once because all traffic passed through a few controlled points.
- **No early warning system:** People had no way to know the shutdown was coming until it was too late.

### What Worked
- **Local networks:** Any system that worked within WiFi or Bluetooth range continued to function.
- **Trust networks:** Information passed between people who knew each other was more reliable than unverified social media posts.
- **Decentralised communication:** When there is no single point of failure, the system cannot be shut down with one switch.

## Why Mukto Mesh Exists

Mukto Mesh is the direct answer to the July 2024 shutdown. Every feature — chat, noticeboard, knowledge base, missing persons, check-in, offline maps — exists because in July 2024, people needed these tools and did not have them.

The next shutdown will not announce itself. The goal of Mukto Mesh is that when it comes, every neighbourhood in Bangladesh will have a node running within minutes.
