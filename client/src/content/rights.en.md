# Know Your Rights

This section covers your legal rights during a crisis in Bangladesh. It is not legal advice. Contact a lawyer for specific legal help.

## If You Are Stopped by Police

- You have the right to ask why you are being stopped.
- If arrested, you must be told the grounds of arrest immediately.
- You have the right to remain silent. You are not required to answer questions beyond giving your name and address.
- Do not resist arrest — even if you believe it is unlawful. Note the officer's name and badge number.

## Rights Upon Arrest

Under Article 33 of the Constitution of Bangladesh:

- **Right to know the grounds:** You must be informed why you are being arrested.
- **Right to a lawyer:** You have the right to consult and be defended by a lawyer of your choice. If you cannot afford one, you are entitled to free legal aid.
- **Right to be produced before a magistrate:** You must be produced before the nearest magistrate within **24 hours** of arrest.
- **Right against illegal detention:** You cannot be held in police custody beyond 24 hours without a magistrate's order. Police custody is limited to a total of **15 days** during investigation.
- **Right to inform family:** The police are required to inform your family of your arrest within 12 hours.

## If Your Device is Searched

- Police generally need a warrant to search your phone or laptop.
- You have the right to remain silent during a search.
- You are not required to provide passwords or unlock your device.
- If a device is seized, ask for a seizure list or receipt.

## Curfew and Movement Restrictions

- During a curfew, you must stay indoors unless you have a valid reason.
- Valid reasons may include: medical emergency, seeking food or water, or going to a shelter.
- If stopped during curfew, explain your situation calmly.
- Curfew orders are temporary. Track the exact timing from your node operator's broadcasts.

## Digital Rights

- Using a VPN is not inherently illegal in Bangladesh, though authorities may block specific VPN protocols during a shutdown.
- The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), which Bangladesh has signed, protects freedom of expression and access to information.
- Creating or sharing verified, factual information about a crisis is protected under freedom of expression.
- Avoid sharing unverified rumours — they can cause panic and endanger lives.

## Women and Children

- **National Helpline for Women:** 109
- **Child Helpline:** 1098
- **Bangladesh Women's Lawyers Association:** Provides free legal support for women in distress.
- **DMP Women's Help Desk:** Available at all police stations in Dhaka.

## If a Family Member is Missing

1. File a general diary (GD) at the nearest police station. Do not wait 24 hours — you can file immediately.
2. Register them on the **People** tab in this app so the community can help.
3. Contact local hospitals and morgues.
4. Reach out to legal aid organisations if you suspect they are in custody.
